Hiring Manager Screen
A 45-minute conversation with the hiring manager, scheduled between the tech screen and the onsite (sometimes also as a team-match gate after onsite). Centered on background fit, role expectations, and the candidate's questions.

Requirements
45-minute video call with the prospective manager.
The manager walks the candidate through the role, the team, and the current roadmap.
The candidate is expected to:
Walk through their background in 5–10 minutes (resume highlights, not a full deep-dive).
Discuss two or three projects at the elevator-pitch level.
Ask 3–5 substantive questions about the team, scope, and growth path.
The output of this round is a binary fit signal for the team-match pool.
Notes
Several candidates report being ghosted for weeks after this round even when the conversation felt strong — the round is gating, not auto-advancing.
"More suitable candidate already in the pipeline" is a common reject reason; the round implicitly weighs background-team fit against the active req's specific needs.
The candidate's questions matter at least as much as the answers. Generic ("what's the team culture") underperforms specific ("how is the team thinking about headcount in the post-AI-tooling era").
A strong question set covers: org & team structure, recent achievement, biggest open problems, role scope, internal tooling, growth trajectory from senior to staff.
The manager may pre-fail a candidate here if they sense the experience scope is below what the req demands — this is the round most likely to surface a downlevel offer.
Preparation
Pre-write a 5-minute background pitch and a 30-second version; rehearse both.
Build a curated question bank organized by theme (org / track record / role / growth / tooling / industry trends). Carry 10–12 questions and pick the 4–5 that fit the manager's intro.
Research the manager's LinkedIn: tenure, prior orgs, public talks. Mention a specific shared interest if it lands naturally; do not force it.
Pre-script answers to: "why are you looking" and "what would make this role compelling for you".
Treat the round as a two-way evaluation; calibration drift toward over-deference reads as low-conviction.
